## 📜 Overview

This project argues that the **2025 National Guard deployment to Washington, D.C.**—justified by a manufactured “crime emergency”—violates the Constitution and existing federal law.  
Through constitutional doctrine, case law, and historical parallels, it demonstrates how **fabricated crises** enable **executive overreach** and reproduce a lineage of **state-sanctioned repression**, from the **Ku Klux Klan** and **Jim Crow** to **COINTELPRO** and contemporary detention regimes.

It advances four core claims:

- **Unconstitutional** under separation of powers, the **Take Care Clause**, the **Posse Comitatus Act**, and constraints of the **Insurrection Act**.  
- **Ultra vires** because **fabricated emergencies** cannot create extraordinary executive power.  
- **Historically continuous** with militarized repression against marginalized communities.  
- **Ethical duty**: Guard members must refuse manifestly unlawful orders.

## 🎯 Highlights & Takeaways

- **Youngstown** places the executive at its **“lowest ebb”** when acting against Congress; fabricated emergencies cannot bypass the PCA/Insurrection Act.  
- **Milligan** and **Duncan** limit military authority where civil courts are open and no imminent public danger exists.  
- **Hamdi** affirms due process even in genuine emergencies; fabricated ones doubly fail.  
- Historical **manufactured crises** (Bonus Army, Kent State, COINTELPRO) show how **false threat narratives** rationalize repression.
