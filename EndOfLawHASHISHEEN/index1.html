Please Enjoy the Review and Reading of this Track from Bill Laswell's Album Hashisheen

In-Depth Study and Analysis of Track 1: “First Reading”

Track Details:
• Title: First Reading
• Duration: 0:29
• Read By: Sussan Deyhim
• Lyrics: “It is no more than two steps to the door of the friend. You are stopping with the first step.”

Introduction:
As the opening track of Hashisheen: The End of Law, “First Reading” sets the tone for an ambitious journey into the intersection of mysticism, history, and avant-garde sound. In just 29 seconds, this piece encapsulates profound philosophical and spiritual themes, serving as an invocation to draw listeners into the world of the Hashshashin.

Through its brevity, the track invokes a sense of mystery and anticipation, prompting listeners to reflect on their own spiritual and intellectual journeys. The minimalistic composition and evocative performance create an atmosphere that is simultaneously intimate and boundless.

Performer Profile: Sussan Deyhim
Artistic and Cultural Significance:
Sussan Deyhim, an Iranian-born vocalist and performance artist, brings authenticity and depth to this opening piece. Known for blending Persian classical music with experimental sounds, her artistry aligns perfectly with the album’s themes of rebellion, mysticism, and transformation.

Key Attributes of Deyhim’s Contribution:
• Cultural Depth: Her roots in Persian classical music and Sufi traditions bring an authenticity that connects this track to its historical and cultural context.
• Experimental Techniques: Deyhim utilizes advanced vocal techniques like overtone singing, microtonal inflections, and glissandos, creating a haunting and otherworldly auditory experience.
• Emotional Resonance: Her vocal delivery is imbued with intensity and reverence, evoking feelings of longing, transcendence, and awe.

Musical Composition and Production

1. Vocals:
• Techniques Used:
• Chant-like intonation reminiscent of Persian spiritual practices.
• Microtonal shifts that evoke the mysticism of Sufi traditions.
• Dynamic modulation to convey layers of emotion and urgency.

2. Instrumentation and Sound Design:
• Ambient Backdrop: Subtle drones or distant percussive elements create a soundscape that feels timeless, anchoring the listener in a liminal space.
• Production Quality: High-fidelity recording ensures every nuance of Deyhim’s performance is preserved, immersing the listener in the spiritual and emotional resonance of the track.

Thematic Analysis:

1. The Door of the Friend:
• Symbolism of "the Friend": In Sufi and Persian mystical traditions, "the Friend" often represents God, the Beloved, or the ultimate truth. It may also refer to a higher state of consciousness, self-realization, or the essence of unconditional love and unity.
• The Door as a Threshold: The door symbolizes the boundary between the material and spiritual realms. To cross it requires a profound transformation and the courage to confront one’s fears and attachments.

2. "Two Steps":
• Simplicity of the Path: The journey to enlightenment is presented as simple yet profound, requiring only two steps:
• Recognizing the call to something greater.
• Taking decisive, transformative action.
• Integration of Dualities: The two steps also symbolize the merging of opposites—self and other, external and internal, worldly and divine. The journey involves transcending these dualities to achieve harmony.

3. "You are stopping with the first step.":
• Self-Imposed Barriers: This line highlights how individuals often hinder their own progress due to fear, doubt, or attachment to comfort zones.
• Fear of Transformation: The second step demands surrender and courage, confronting the possibility of losing one’s current identity for something greater.
• Unfulfilled Potential: The lyric is a call to action, reminding the listener that acknowledgment alone is not enough; action is essential.

Philosophical Context:
• Sufi Influences: The phrase resonates deeply with Sufi teachings, where the "Friend" symbolizes union with God. Crossing "the door" is the ultimate goal of the spiritual seeker, achieved through love, surrender, and self-purification.
• Universal Application: Beyond spirituality, the phrase applies to any journey of personal growth or achievement. Whether pursuing a dream, resolving a conflict, or deepening a relationship, success requires moving beyond initial intentions and taking courageous, decisive actions.

Reflective Questions:
• How does the simplicity of the phrase “two steps” challenge the way we perceive spiritual or personal growth?
• What internal barriers prevent you from taking the second step in your own life?
• How does the interplay of traditional and experimental elements shape your understanding of the track’s themes?

Conclusion:
"First Reading" is a powerful invocation that encapsulates the themes of Hashisheen: The End of Law within its concise 29-second runtime. Through Sussan Deyhim’s evocative performance and Bill Laswell’s atmospheric production, the track introduces listeners to a journey that is as personal as it is universal.

Its central lyric—“It is no more than two steps to the door of the friend. You are stopping with the first step.”—serves as a timeless reminder of the courage required to overcome our own limitations and embrace the unknown. As the opening to the album, it invites introspection and sets the stage for the profound explorations that follow.
